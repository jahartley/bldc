# WRSM STARTER-GENERATOR DIAGNOSTICS REFERENCE MANUAL
This document is the definitive engineering reference for configuring, analyzing, and tuning the Wound Rotor Synchronous Machine (WRSM) starter-generator control loops using our custom 1 kHz cycle-accurate diagnostic logger.

The logging engine is split into two distinct physical phases using a contiguous two-element array:
- Phase 0: Acceleration/Ramp Phase (phases[0]): High-transient window where the motor fights static friction, rotating inertia, and initial cylinder compressions to climb the linear speed ramp.
- Phase 1: Hold RPM Phase (phases[1]): Steady-state window where the motor holds a constant cranking speed, fighting cyclic engine compression pulses prior to fuel ignition.

## CORE CONTROL & PID TUNING METRICS
### Speed Error (ERPM): Max vs. Average (The Speed Crest Factor)
What they are:
- Max Speed Error: The largest single speed tracking error observed during that phase.
- Average Speed Error: The mathematical mean of the tracking error over the entire phase duration.

How to calculate the Speed Crest Factor:
- Crest Factor = Max Speed Error / Average Speed Error

What the ratio reveals:
- High Ratio (> 3.5): Your speed loop is stable overall, but you are experiencing sudden, localized disturbances. This is the ideal signature when crossing Top Dead Center (TDC) compression strokes; the motor slows down momentarily at TDC but holds its ground elsewhere.
- Low Ratio (< 1.5) with high absolute values: The speed loop is under-damped and sluggish. The motor is drifting away from the target speed and staying off-target.
- Tuning Action: Raise your proportional gain (s_pid_kp) to increase the "stiffness" of the speed loop.

### Proportional Error (P-Term): Max vs. Average
What they are:
- Max P-Term Error: The largest output contribution from the Proportional block of the speed controller (Normalized 0.0 to 1.0).
- Average P-Term Error: The mean contribution of the Proportional block over the active phase.

What the ratio reveals:
- Average close to Peak (Ratio < 1.3): Kp is continuously saturated and over-active. The controller is violently swinging the current command trying to correct minor speed fluctuations, which will cause overheating and oscillation once the engine breaks free.
- Tuning Action: Reduce s_pid_kp immediately and let the preloaded Integrator (Ki) do the heavy lifting.

### Peak I-Term Output (Normalized: 0.0 to 1.0)
What it is: 
- The peak output contribution from the Integral (Ki) block of the speed controller.

What it means: 
- Measures how much sustained, accumulated effort the controller is applying to maintain speed.

Tuning Action:
- If value hits 1.0 during Ramp: The integrator is fully winding up. Your start-ramp is too steep.
- If value is very low (< 0.2) but Speed Error is high: Your integral gain (s_pid_ki) is too low. Raise s_pid_ki.

### Max Duty Cycle (%)
What it is: 
- The maximum PWM modulation depth reached by the stator power stage.

What it means: 
- Shows how close the VESC is to running out of battery voltage headroom to drive stator current.

Tuning Action:
- If Max Duty Cycle hits 95% to 99%: The motor has hit "voltage saturation". The battery voltage is too low to push the commanded current through the motor windings at this speed. To fix this, you must either raise battery bus voltage, decrease the target cranking speed, or reduce the rotor field current (to lower back-EMF).

## POWER, TORQUE & EFFICIENCY METRICS
### Peak Torque, Peak Magnetic Torque, and Peak Reluctance Torque (Nm)
What they are: 
- Instantaneous electromagnetic torque values calculated at 1 kHz using live stator currents (Iq, Id) and live rotor flux (lambda).
- Magnetic Torque is generated by stator current pulling on the rotor's excited flux.
- Reluctance Torque is generated by the rotor's claw-pole steel aligning with the stator's magnetic path due to rotor saliency (Ld != Lq).

What they mean: 
- Maps the exact mechanical torque demanded to spin your specific engine.
Tuning Action:
- Reluctance Harvesting: If Reluctance Torque is significant (e.g., >10% of total) during the high-load ramp, you can increase system efficiency by injecting a small negative D-axis stator current (Id). This pulls "free" reluctance torque from the claw-pole steel, allowing you to reduce Q-axis current (Iq) and lower inverter heat.
- Compression Profiling: As you add spark plugs, watch the gap between "Min Torque" and "Max Torque" during Hold. This delta represents your raw cylinder compression torque signature.
### Peak & Average R_sys (DC System Resistance in mOhms)
What it is: 
- The live calculated DC resistance of your entire high-current electrical path (Battery internal resistance + wiring + contactors + VESC traces).
Calculated via: R_sys = (V_rest - V_bus) / I_battery

What it means: 
- Measures the electrical bottleneck and health of your power delivery system.

Tuning Action:
- If R_sys is between 5 and 15 mOhms: Exceptional. Your cabling, terminals, and battery pack are properly sized.
- If R_sys is above 20 mOhms: Critical warning. You have thin battery leads, loose ring terminals, pitting contactors, or a weak battery cell. Massive current draws will cause extreme sags, risking a VESC brownout/reboot mid-crank.
- Thermal Calculation: If R_sys reads 32 mOhms and you draw 200A, you are dissipating:
Power_loss = I^2 * R = (200)^2 * 0.032 = 1280 Watts of waste heat. This will rapidly heat connections and board copper planes.

### Average Electrical In Power vs. Mechanical Out Power & System Efficiency (%)
What they are: 
- Real-time electrical power drawn from the battery versus physical rotational power delivered to the crankshaft. 
- Efficiency = (Mechanical Power / Electrical Power) * 100

What they mean: 
- Measures the overall electromechanical conversion efficiency of the WRSM.

Tuning Action:
- If Efficiency is high (>65%): Your Ortega observer angle tracking is perfectly aligned with the physical rotor flux, and your rotor pre-excitation current is well-balanced.
- If Efficiency is low (<40%): You are dumping massive energy as waste heat. This indicates either:
  - The observer angle is drifting under heavy loads (verify your Ld/Lq lookup tables).
  - Your rotor field is magnetically saturated (you are pumping too much current into the rotor coil for negligible flux returns). Try reducing rotor current from 2.5A to 2.0A.

## MECHANICAL INTEGRITY & STABILITY METRICS (HOLD PHASE)
### Peak-Peak Speed Ripple (ERPM)
What it is: 
- The difference between the maximum and minimum speed observed during the constant Hold phase.

What it means: 
- Measures the mechanical speed fluctuations caused by cylinder compression pulses.

Tuning Action:
- If Speed Ripple is too high (>350 ERPM): The speed loop is under-damped and hunting. Reduce s_pid_ki to stop the controller from over-correcting on compression strokes.
- If Speed Ripple is normal (100 to 250 ERPM): Perfect. This represents the healthy, natural speed variation of a single or multi-cylinder engine crossing Top Dead Center (TDC).

### Belt Slip Alert
What triggers it: 
- Speed Ripple drops to near-zero (<40 ERPM) while average stator current (Avg Iq) remains high (>120A) during the Hold Phase.

What it means: 
- The rubber starter belt has lost traction and is slipping on the MGU pulley.

The Physics: 
- A slipping belt acts as a mechanical low-pass filter, spinning smoothly on the belt back and shielding the FOC observer from the engine's physical compression pulses.

Tuning Action: 
- Do not change PID gains. Mechanically tighten your bracket mount, increase belt tension, or increase pulley belt wrap.
